- question: How should Microsoft 365 workspace ownership be assigned for audit readiness?
  answer: Assign a named owner when every team, site or group is created, and set
    a fallback if that owner leaves. This answers ownership questions directly.
- question: How do recurring Microsoft 365 access reviews support audit evidence?
  answer: Owners should confirm access and purpose at a set interval, while inactive
    workspaces are archived. Each grant confirmation or removal becomes a dated record.
- question: What Microsoft 365 reports should be scheduled before an audit?
  answer: Schedule recurring reports covering inventory, violations and review results.
    Reports can be delivered daily, weekly or monthly and exported when requested.

## TL;DR

Microsoft 365 audit readiness requires a named owner for every workspace, recurring reviews of access and activity, a central record of decisions, and scheduled reports on inventory, violations and review results. These five steps replace last-minute evidence gathering with current records that can be exported when auditors ask.

## Governance takeaway

- Admins/CoE: Require an owner when each team, site or group is created, with a fallback owner, so ownership questions need no later research.
- Security/Compliance: Group audit requests around ownership, access, review and removal, then retain dated decisions as evidence that controls ran.
- Leadership/Business: Prioritize sensitive or externally shared workspaces and treat ongoing evidence collection as routine operations, because unmanaged spaces create avoidable audit work.
