## TL;DR

Michael Roth reframes the governance pitch per audience instead of leading with rules: to makers, it's "making sure your apps don't break when you're on vacation"; to managers, rules work "like traffic lights" that speed things up rather than slow them down; to IT, governance beats shadow IT by giving visibility without anyone becoming the villain.

{% include curated-attribution.html %}

## Governance takeaway

- Leadership/Business: test the next governance announcement against Roth's traffic-light framing before sending it — a rule pitched as a speed bump gets read as a speed bump regardless of what it actually does.
- Makers: respond to the continuity argument specifically — "your app won't break while you're on vacation" recruits more real compliance than the same rule framed as a restriction.
- Admins/CoE: get the sharpest version of the IT angle — visibility beats policing as the pitch against shadow IT, since nobody signs up to be cast as the villain.
